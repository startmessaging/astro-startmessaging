Meera runs customer support for a Jaipur home-furnishings brand that sells on its own site and on marketplaces. Her team handles roughly 12,000 WhatsApp conversations a month: sizing questions, delivery delays, return pickups. Until September, the line on the Meta invoice covering all those agent replies read ₹0. When the October bill lands in early November, it will not. The WhatsApp Business API pricing change on 1 October 2026 turned two kinds of message that used to be free into billable ones, and businesses without a payment method on their WhatsApp account can find template sends simply fail.

This is not a rate increase dressed up as news. It is a change to *what counts as billable*, and the impact lands hardest on exactly the teams that did what Meta encouraged: reply to customers inside the 24-hour window.

## What Changed on 1 October 2026

Two things changed. Everything else about the WhatsApp Business Platform, including the 24-hour customer service window, template approval and the category system, works the way it did before.

### Utility templates are charged inside the 24-hour window

From 1 July 2025, utility templates sent inside an open 24-hour window were free. That waiver ended on 1 October 2026. Meta now charges every delivered template, marketing, utility and authentication, wherever it lands. An order confirmation or shipping update that cost nothing in September because it went out during an active conversation is now billed at Meta's utility rate for the recipient's country.

That also closes the obvious workaround. You cannot dodge a service charge by converting a free-form reply into a utility template, because the template is charged too.

### Service replies are free only up to a monthly allowance

A service message is any free-form, non-template message your business sends inside an open 24-hour customer service window: an agent typing "Let me check the courier status for you," a chatbot's "Thanks, we've received your query," or anything an AI assistant sends on your behalf. These had been free since late 2024.

Since 1 October 2026, service replies are free up to a monthly allowance per business phone number. Past that allowance, Meta charges them at its service rate. Two details matter. The allowance is per *phone number*, not per WhatsApp Business Account, so a brand running separate numbers for retail, wholesale and after-sales has a separate allowance on each. And service messages get no volume discount once you are past it.

Meta publishes the allowance size and the India rates for every category on its [official WhatsApp pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing). We do not reproduce the figures here because Meta revises them; check the current card against your invoice rather than trusting any summary, including this one.

## How to Read the India Rate Card Now

India is billed in rupees, so the India card gives you literal invoice numbers. After 1 October, read it with three questions in mind.

### Which categories you actually send

Marketing is priced well above utility and authentication in India, and that gap matters more than any single rate. If you have transactional messages sitting in marketing templates, that is still the biggest saving available, October change or not.

### How much of your traffic is in-window

The new costs come from two places: utility templates you send inside an open window and service replies beyond the free allowance. Teams with heavy support traffic feel the change most; teams that mostly send marketing campaigns barely notice it.

### What tax does

Meta's charges carry GST on your invoice. Plan for a GST-bearing line item that did not exist in September, and confirm input tax credit treatment with your CA.

## What Is Still Free

The change is narrower than the headlines suggest, and it helps to know exactly where the free ground still is.

Incoming messages remain free. You are billed only for messages your business delivers, so a customer sending you fourteen messages in a row costs you nothing. The 24-hour customer service window itself works the same way: each inbound customer message reopens it, and inside it you can still send free-form replies without a template. Those replies are free up to the monthly allowance and metered after it.

The 72-hour free entry point window survives too. When a customer writes in from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad or a Facebook Page button and you reply in time, Meta opens a 72-hour window in which it charges nothing for messages in that chat, templates included. If a meaningful share of your inbound volume comes from paid social, that traffic is now materially cheaper to service than organic inbound. The window does not replace the 24-hour rule: once the customer's 24 hours have passed, you still need an approved template to message them.

## Working Out Your Own Bill

Back to Meera. Her team's 12,000 monthly conversations generate about six outbound free-form messages each: three from the automated greeting-and-triage flow, three from a human agent. That is 72,000 service messages a month, almost all of them beyond any monthly free allowance. Her order flow also sends around 15,000 utility templates a month inside open windows, which were free until 30 September.

The arithmetic is simple once you have the inputs:

| Line item | What to count | How to price it |
|---|---|---|
| Service replies | Outbound free-form messages per number per month, minus the free allowance | Meta's India service rate |
| In-window utility templates | Utility templates sent while a 24-hour window was open | Meta's India utility rate |
| Tax | Sum of the above | Add GST |

Run that on your own numbers with Meta's current India rates. The two inputs you need are your monthly count of outbound free-form messages per number and your count of utility templates that go out inside an open window. Both are available in WhatsApp Manager and your provider's message logs. If your platform cannot separate in-window utility sends from out-of-window ones, raise that with them now rather than after the first metered invoice.

## The Post-1 October Checklist

If you did not get through this before the deadline, do it this week.

- **Add or verify a payment method** on your WhatsApp Business Account in WhatsApp Manager, and confirm the card or mandate is not expiring in the next quarter. Without a valid payment method, template sends fail.
- **Confirm who Meta bills.** If a provider is billed by Meta and invoices you, the payment method sits with them; if Meta bills your own WhatsApp Business Account, it sits with you. Get this in writing.
- **Set a spend alert** so the first metered month does not run past what finance expected.
- **Audit your automated flows** for messages that were free and now cost money: auto-acknowledgements, "an agent will be with you shortly" holds, post-resolution satisfaction pings.
- **Brief your finance team** that the October invoice carries a new line item.

Meera's team found the expensive part in the fourth item. Their triage bot sent three messages before a human ever joined the chat. Trimming it to a single message with quick-reply buttons removes 24,000 billable messages a month, a third of her service volume, for one afternoon of work.

## Five Ways to Cut the New Service Bill

Billing is per message, not per conversation. That single fact reshapes what good support hygiene looks like.

- **Send fewer, fuller messages.** An agent who answers in one complete message instead of three fragments cuts that conversation's service cost by two-thirds. Four outbound messages per conversation instead of six takes a third off Meera's service bill.
- **Cut automated filler.** Acknowledgements, typing-indicator substitutes and "still there?" nudges were harmless when free. Each is now a charge across every conversation you handle once the allowance is used up.
- **Use buttons and lists properly.** One interactive message carrying five options is one billable message; five sequential questions are five. Our [WhatsApp message template guide](/blog/top-whatsapp-message-template-tips) covers how to structure these so they still convert.
- **Route paid-social traffic through Click-to-WhatsApp.** Those chats get the 72-hour free entry point window, so the same support interaction costs less when it starts from an ad.
- **Re-examine your OTP channel honestly.** A [WhatsApp authentication template](/whatsapp/authentication) only reaches users who have WhatsApp installed, and it fails differently from SMS. SMS reaches every handset on Jio, Airtel, Vi and BSNL with no app dependency. Our breakdown of [SMS OTP versus WhatsApp OTP](/blog/sms-otp-vs-whatsapp-otp) covers where each wins. StartMessaging's India SMS is a separate product at a flat ₹0.25 per message, with no DLT registration to manage; if you use both channels, your app calls each API on its own.

What none of this should become is a reason to reply less to customers. The teams that will regret October are the ones that respond to a per-message charge by making support harder to reach. A reply that saves a ₹4,000 return is not a cost problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What exactly is a WhatsApp service message?**

A: Any free-form, non-template message your business sends to a customer inside the open 24-hour customer service window, whether from a human agent, your own chatbot or an AI assistant. Template messages are classified separately as marketing, utility or authentication and billed under those categories.

**Q: Are service messages still free?**

A: Up to a point. Since 1 October 2026, service replies are free up to a monthly allowance per business phone number and charged after that. Each number has its own allowance. Meta lists the allowance and the rates on its [pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing).

**Q: Are utility templates free inside the 24-hour window?**

A: Not any more. Meta charges every delivered utility template since 1 October 2026, inside or outside the window. Moving the send time saves nothing; sending fewer utility messages does.

**Q: What happens if there is no payment method on my WhatsApp account?**

A: Meta refuses template sends until a valid payment method is added in WhatsApp Manager. On StartMessaging, the API answers `402 META_PAYMENT_REQUIRED` in that case, and the Connect page shows a "Payment method added in Meta" checklist item. If a provider is billed by Meta on your behalf, confirm in writing that their payment method is current.

**Q: Is any WhatsApp traffic still entirely free?**

A: Yes. Incoming messages are free, and when a customer writes in from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad or a Facebook Page button and you reply in time, Meta charges nothing for messages in that chat for 72 hours, templates included.
